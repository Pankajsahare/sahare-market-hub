Strategy Playbook

Twelve structures with payoff shapes — load, then reshape.

Long Call

Bullish

Buy one at-the-money call. Unlimited upside, and the premium paid is the entire worst case.

Max loss: premium · Max gain: unlimited

Long Put

Bearish

Buy one at-the-money put. Sharp falls pay you; time decay is the cost of the insurance.

Max loss: premium · Large downside gain

Bull Call Spread

Bullish

Buy the near call and sell a higher strike against it. Cheaper than a naked call, with a defined ceiling.

Defined risk · Defined reward

Bear Put Spread

Bearish

Buy the near put and finance it by selling a lower strike — a measured way to lean short.

Defined risk · Defined reward

Long Straddle

Volatility

Buy the ATM call and put together. Direction is irrelevant — you need a move big enough to beat both premiums.

Max loss: premiums · Gain: unlimited

Long Strangle

Volatility

The straddle's cheaper cousin: an OTM call plus an OTM put. Lower cost, but the move must be larger.

Max loss: premiums · Gain: unlimited

Iron Condor

Income

Sell an OTM put spread and an OTM call spread. Collect theta while price stays between the short strikes.

Defined risk · Credit income

Iron Butterfly

Income

A short ATM straddle wrapped in protective wings. Fat credit, best outcome if price pins the strike.

Defined risk · Narrow profit tent

Call Butterfly

Neutral

Buy one lower call, sell two ATM, buy one higher. A cheap, precise bet on where price will settle.

Small debit · Sharp profit tent

Calendar Call

Time

Sell the near-dated ATM call and buy the far-dated one to harvest faster front-month decay.

Debit risk · Profits from term decay

Jade Lizard

Income

Short OTM put plus a short OTM call spread. If the credit exceeds the call width, upside risk disappears.

Downside risk only · No upside risk if credit > width

Call Ratio 1×2

Advanced

Buy one near call, sell two far calls. Often near zero cost — but a runaway rally hurts. Keep size small.

Unlimited upside risk · Manage size

Options Strategy FAQ

What is an iron condor and when does it work?

An iron condor sells an out-of-the-money put spread and an out-of-the-money call spread on the same expiry. It collects a net credit and keeps most of it if the underlying stays between the two short strikes until expiry. Both wings cap the worst case, so risk is defined. It works best when implied volatility is high and you expect a range, not a trend.

Why does coin-settled P&L differ from cash P&L?

Deribit BTC and ETH options are inverse: premium and settlement are paid in the coin, not in dollars. The cash value of an expiring call is still strike-to-spot in dollars, but the coin amount is that value divided by the settlement price. So in coin terms a call's upside flattens as price rises, while a put's downside grows faster as price falls. Switch the denomination toggle to see the number your exchange account will actually show.

How is probability of profit calculated here?

The tool assumes a lognormal price distribution at the at-the-money implied volatility of the payoff horizon expiry, with the risk-free rate used for the forward drift. It then adds up the probability of all price regions where the expiry payoff is positive after fees. It is an estimate, not a forecast: real markets have fatter tails and volatility changes.

What does the P&L heatmap show?

Each column is a possible underlying price and each row is a number of days forward. The colour is the model P&L at that price and time, using your IV shift. The dotted white curve marks zero P&L, the amber line is current spot, and the ring is your active scenario. Click a cell, or focus the heatmap and use arrow keys plus Enter, to steer the scenario dials.

Is the NIFTY and BANKNIFTY data live?

Only if a data proxy has been configured by the site owner. Indian exchange feeds do not allow direct browser requests, so without a proxy the index chain is a clearly labelled simulation built from a seeded spot, a volatility smile and current lot sizes. Never place a trade from simulated numbers — verify the live chain with your broker first.
